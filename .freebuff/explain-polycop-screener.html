🛡️ Visual explainer · PolyCop Bot Screener

Find Polymarket wallets whose edge survives being copied on a $100 bankroll.

PolyCop Bot Screener is an automated screening, scoring and simulation engine that answers one question the public leaderboard can't: this wallet made money — but would I make money copying it, trading later, smaller, and at worse prices? A three-phase pipeline scrapes every leaderboard wallet, filters the copyable few through hard rejection gates plus a 100-point audit, then replays each survivor through a simulation of your exact copy-bot configuration to issue the final verdict.

01 · The core insight

Profitability and copyability are different properties

The leaderboard measures what a wallet made. It says nothing about what you would make following it. Your copy arrives later, is a fraction of the size, pays the venue's minimum-order bump on small trades, gets clipped by position caps on big ones, and eats real slippage the leaderboard's model understates by a factor of 4.2 (ADR 0001). The screener exists to separate wallets whose edge survives all of that — copy targets — from the ones that only look profitable.

✅ Copyability (what the screener measures)

The property of an edge surviving replication by a follower who trades later, smaller and at worse prices. The canonical mirage shapes the engine was built to catch:

🤖
The arbitrage bot
Wins by reacting in milliseconds across venues. Its whole edge is latency — the one thing a copybot never inherits. A follower trading seconds later buys the wrong side of the arbitrage.
🎰
The short lucky streak
20 hot trades against a lifetime of mediocrity. In lifetime numbers a dead edge and an edge that never existed look identical — which is why Recent Form is judged against the friction it came through.

💎 The Hidden Gem (why the screen exists)

By definition, a wallet the screen rates highly while the leaderboard's own score rates poorly (site score < 75, screener A-Tier ≥ 71). The two disagree because the site's score optimises for something other than copyability. That's exactly why Phase 1 scrapes every profile with no site-score pre-filter — filtering low scorers early would make gems unfindable. The engine keeps its own sanity floor instead.

Leaderboard rank: #340  ·  Site score 61 / 100
Screener verdict: A-Tier (74 pts), passes all 8 gates
"Rated highly" is defined by the recalibrated A-Tier floor — the old hard-coded 80 is gone so the gem definition can't silently diverge from the tier bands (ADR 0005/0010).
02 · Anatomy of a scan

One scan = scrape → triage → simulate, written as three coupled phases

A scan is the complete pass: every candidate scraped, filtered, scored and simulated together, producing the feed the page reads. Each phase writes a JSON file the next phase reads — which also means each phase can run standalone against a fixture (exactly how the tests exercise them). screen.py and the web app's /api/rescan both call the same run_pipeline() orchestrator, so adding a phase touches one call site, not two.

03 · The 100-Point Audit Engine

Eight gates you can't buy your way past, then ten weighted parameters

A Hard Rejection Gate is binary and never traded off against points earned elsewhere. Only after passing every gate does a candidate get scored. Crucially, a parameter whose input could not be measured contributes nothing — absent stays absent (None ≠ 0), so an unmeasured wallet ranks below a measured poor one, never above it. All gate values, weights and bands are generated from SCORING_SPEC into these docs by tools/scoring_docs.py; CI fails if the two drift.

04 · The simulation decides

The Slippage Sensitivity Sweep: the primary mirage test

ADR 0002 demoted the 100-point score to a cheap pre-filter: the verdict comes from replaying each survivor's transaction history through your exact Copy Execution Profile, at rising friction. Genuine edge degrades gradually; a mirage inverts. Edge Retention = PnL at 10% slippage ÷ PnL at 2% slippage — the fraction of profit that survives the trip from the leaderboard's optimistic assumption to the observed one. Gate ordering inside the sweep: a failed fetch at a gated level is an endpoint failure (ADR 0007) — rejected, never a fabricated $0. A measured non-positive PnL at 10% or 2% is a measured rejection. 05 · Copy Execution Profile

A verdict is valid only for the follower's exact bot config

Every number that depends on how your bot executes lives in one frozen dataclass, CopyExecutionProfile — bankroll, copy ratio, slippage assumption, price window, capital caps, venue minimum. The screen states the profile beside every result, keyed by a SHA-256 fingerprint: change a setting and every cached verdict misses rather than silently serving a result computed under a profile that no longer exists. Defaults below, straight from the code:

Bankroll
$100
Copy ratio
3%
Per-trade copy
$3.00
Max single position
$5.00
Venue min order
$1.00
Price window
$0.05 – $0.95
The Copyable Trade Window — the range of target trade sizes your bot actually mirrors. Outside it the realised copy ratio stops matching the nominal one, so those trades are ignored entirely.

🔒 Frozen & fingerprinted

The profile is immutable and its SHA-256 fingerprint keys every cache file. Results carry copy_execution_profile + fingerprint in the JSON, so provenance is never guessed.

📐 Derived, never hand-picked

Window bounds and the Sizing Fit peak are computed from the profile (window_min_usd, window_max_usd, sizing_fit_peak_usd). Move a cap or ratio and the peak follows.

💥 Fails loud at startup

copy_ratio ≤ 0 raises immediately. A typo in the settings surfaces as a clear error instead of a ZeroDivisionError deep inside scoring.

06 · Honesty discipline

The engine refuses to guess — three rules run through everything

🟡 Absent stays absent

None is not 0. A missing figure is unmeasured, so it scores nothing and renders as "Not measured" — never as a convenient zero. For a cost, zero is the best possible reading, so coercing absence to zero would hand out free points (a previous engine version gave every candidate a free 44).

r20_slip = None → Recent Form = 0 pts
# not "slippage was free"

🔴 Failures degrade the scan, honestly

One wallet the endpoint won't answer → that wallet is rejected with an endpoint-failure reason, the scan continues. Three consecutive failures → an outage: stop hammering the dead endpoint, keep the verdicts already computed, fall the unreached back to triage order, label every row's verdict_source.

consecutive_failures ≥ 3
→ reduced_confidence = True
→ unreached → triage order

🟢 Atomic, visible publishes

The feed is written to a .tmp then moved onto the real file with os.replace, so a polling reader never sees a half-written scan. A publish failure raises instead of being swallowed — silence is what let a broken publish path go unnoticed for a whole day of "successful" scans.

json.dump(tmp) → os.replace(tmp, feed)
# or raise — never fake success
07 · The web app

A zero-build dashboard over a std-lib-only Python server

screen.py decides whether a scan is needed, opens your browser, and starts an http.server proxy on port 8050. The browser does no scoring — everything on the page is precomputed by the pipeline, because a second engine in the browser could disagree with the first, and the number on screen is the one people act on. Two pages:

📊 Trader Screener

Wallet cards ranked by simulated performance (Edge Retention default). Each row shows triage score and simulated verdict — and says "Not simulated" when a simulation never ran, so a triage grade is never dressed up as a verdict. The detail modal shows the 10-parameter score breakdown, a performance radar, Balance Miss (trades the $100 bankroll couldn't fund — the one figure on the page about the follower, not the target) and Skip Reasons from the simulation's decision log.

Sidebar filters: activity recency buckets (Live <6h → Stale 7d+), Hidden Gems only, and "Tight sizing only" (order floor ≤ $25).

⚡ Latency & Slippage Profiler

A live, budgeted benchmark (14-second hard cap) against the real Polymarket CLOB: HTTP RTT to /book, WebSocket RTT to the orderbook feed, and an orderbook VWAP slippage matrix across active 5m and 15m markets at trade sizes $10→$250. A 5-tier gauge classifies latency; partial runs say they're partial.

Why it exists: the sweep simulates slippage as an input, but your real fill also pays latency. This page measures the number your execution profile's slippage assumption stands in for.

🔌 Server routes

GET /api/leaderboard — CORS proxy to polycop.fun, so the page can scrape live without mixed-content issues
GET /api/rescan — runs the full 3-phase pipeline server-side and returns the fresh feed
GET /api/clear_data — resets the cached dataset files
GET /api/measure_latency — the budgeted live latency + slippage benchmark
GET /data/* → app/data · everything else → app/web (no-cache headers throughout)
Entry points: screen.py (launcher), app/src/pipeline/orchestrator.py (one call site for phase1→2→3), serve_web_app.py (server).
08 · Design decisions

Every threshold here is a recorded decision, not a guess

The repo keeps an ADR log — ten decisions so far, each with consequences and rejected alternatives. The ones that shape the engine:

ADR 0001

Friction Realism Multiplier = 4.2

Logged live fills showed 5.8%–14.9% slippage where the leaderboard models ~2%. Every leaderboard slippage figure is treated as understated ×4.2; the slippage gate is set at 5% modelled (≈21% real). A tracked estimate, expected to move as fills accumulate.

ADR 0002

Simulation ranks; the score only triages

The copyability score is demoted to a cheap pre-filter deciding who's worth simulating. Tiers come from simulated performance. Results cache against a profile fingerprint, not the wallet alone.

ADR 0003

Recent-20 win-rate gate dropped

As a standalone hard gate it rejected the wrong wallets; the divergence and P/L gates cover the failure mode instead. The metric remains visible for reference.

ADR 0004

Recent Form measures return on deployed capital

Absolute dollars reward the target's size; size is what the trade window constrains. Full marks = ≥100% return over the recent-20 window, discounted by the friction it was earned through.

ADR 0005 → 0010

Tier bands calibrated on real data, re-measured after the reweight

Copyability tier floors calibrated against a real scored run (0005), then re-scaled after the 11-parameter reweight shifted the distribution (0010) so an S-Tier's share of survivors couldn't triple under stale floors.

ADR 0006

Copyable Window Share is simulation-only

No leaderboard field can proxy a distributional share of entry signals, so its former 10 triage points were redistributed. The metric lives in the simulation where it can be measured properly.

ADR 0007

A failed fetch is absent, not zero

Endpoint failures record None in pnl_by_level and reject with an endpoint-failure reason — a network failure and a genuine break-even stay distinguishable everywhere downstream.

ADR 0008

Sustained failure degrades the scan

A streak of unanswerable wallets is an outage, not a flake: stop, keep computed verdicts, fall the unreached back to triage order at reduced confidence — and label every row's verdict source.

ADR 0009

Verdict metrics come from the simulation

The page's Daily Green Rate, Drawdown and skip reasons describe the follower's simulated account, not the target's leaderboard lifetime — all from the same 10% run that produces the verdict PnL.

09 · Glossary

The vocabulary the code enforces

CONTEXT.md defines a strict domain language — and the code avoids the banned terms, because a "backtest" that isn't a backtest misleads its reader. Tap to expand.

Copy Target
A candidate that survived every hard rejection gate. Only targets are scored and simulated.
Avoid: signal provider, guru, trader
Mirage
A wallet whose surface statistics look excellent but whose edge cannot survive being copied. Canonical shapes: the arbitrage bot and the short lucky streak.
Avoid: fake, scam, bad wallet
Hard Rejection Gate
A binary disqualifier applied before scoring, never traded off against points earned elsewhere.
Avoid: filter, threshold, cutoff
Copyability Score
A weighted sum used to triage candidates cheaply. It orders wallets for further work; it is not the verdict. Unmeasured inputs contribute nothing.
Avoid: rating, grade, rank
Tier
A letter band presenting a verdict to the reader. Tiers derive from simulated performance, not from the copyability score.
Avoid: grade, class
Modelled Copy PnL
The leaderboard's counterfactual profit under a fixed optimistic friction assumption and a full-size mirror. A thin-margin detector, not a follower's expected outcome.
Avoid: backtest copy PnL, copy PnL
Edge-to-Friction Ratio
A target's average round-trip edge divided by expected round-trip friction. Below 1 the wallet is uncopyable by arithmetic, regardless of any other metric — hence the 24-point weight and full-marks at 3×.
Copyable Trade Window
The range of target trade sizes the execution profile actually mirrors; trades outside it are ignored entirely. Below the window the venue minimum bumps the copy up (over-exposure); above it the position cap clips it (under-mirror).
Balance Miss
A target trade the simulation could not follow because the bankroll was fully deployed — direct evidence of capital exhaustion at a given account size.
Avoid: missed trade, skip
Tracking Error
Divergence between the target's results and the follower's. Never random — it concentrates in whichever trades the execution profile handles worst.
Avoid: drift, divergence, slippage
Friction Realism Multiplier
The factor (4.2) by which observed execution friction exceeds the leaderboard model's assumption. Calibrated from logged live fills; expected to move as fills accumulate.
Scan
A complete screening pass — every candidate scraped, filtered, scored and simulated together, producing the feed the page reads. A repeated scan is a rescan.
Avoid: run, audit run, pipeline run
